The build

Step 1 · The teacher's voice — Question 1: "How do I keep my style?" Create CLAUDE.md with the teacher's rules and examples: who the materials are for, tone, simple and inexpensive activities, clear instructions, clean layout, Spanish and English side by side. Paste in one real example activity. Say: "The memory file is how the AI learns the teacher's style instead of inventing one. Same trick as the September tips."

Step 2 · The generator — Question 6: "Drafts by age, theme, skill and language?" Define the inputs (age 1–5, theme, skills, language) and have Claude draft one week as structured data: objectives, five daily activities, materials, vocabulary list in both languages. Ask the room for a theme.

Step 3 · The printable pack — Brief: "ready to print, letter size" Turn the plan into HTML pages: cover, instructions for the teacher or family, weekly plan, activity pages (counting, vocabulary, art, motor). Print to PDF and show it.

Step 4 · Quality checks — Question 5: "Original, good quality, age-appropriate?" Add a review checklist and have Claude check the draft against it: age-appropriate tasks and safety (no small parts under 3), correct Spanish and English, nothing copied, images safe to sell. A person signs off on every page. Ask a Spanish speaker in the room to review the Spanish.

Step 5 · Making many — Question 2: "Create, review and organize many printables efficiently?" Generate a month of themes into an organized folder structure (theme/age/week), each with a review status, so the teacher reviews a batch instead of starting from blank pages.

Step 6 · Selling and promotion — Questions 3 and 4: "Automate publishing and promotion?" and "Buy and download without me sending it?" Draft the product title, description and preview images for the store; draft three Pinterest pin texts. Explain that Teachers Pay Teachers and Etsy deliver digital files automatically after purchase; the teacher uploads once. Mention the current marketplace rules you checked.

If it stalls

Narrate what Claude is doing; if a step fails, open the matching part of the fallback pack and keep going. Don't debug a single attendee's laptop from the front: "pair with your neighbor; I'll come by."

Questions you may get

Question Short answer
"Is it allowed to sell AI-assisted content?" Depends on the marketplace and changes over time; read the current rules and disclose where required. The teacher's review and editing is what makes it the teacher's work.
"Who owns the result?" Keep it original: the teacher's ideas, edited by the teacher, with images you have rights to.
"Can this run without the cloud?" Drafting can use local models, but quality drops; the printing and packaging are all local.
"Why not just use Canva?" Canva is great for one page. The value here is generating a consistent pack by age, theme and skill, many at a time, in two languages.
